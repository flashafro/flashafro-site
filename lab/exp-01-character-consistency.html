The Lab

Building AI Characters

Midjourney Adobe Firefly Character reference Style reference Claude (concept)
Brief

What I was testing

Can you create AI-generated characters that feel like real, distinct people — not just archetypes or mood board exports? Specifically: could I define a character's personality, backstory, and visual identity clearly enough that a generative image model would produce consistent, recognisable results across many different scenes and contexts?

This was the foundational experiment. Everything else in the lab builds on whether this is actually achievable.

Process

How I approached it

I started by writing character bibles — the kind of document you'd write for a TV show character, not an image generator. Age, background, specific quirks, emotional register, the way they hold themselves in a room. I found that the specificity of the writing directly influenced the specificity of the output.

Then came the visual anchoring phase: running dozens of generations to find images that felt right, then using those as character references to lock down the look. Not one reference — three to five, from different angles and contexts, to give the model a rounded sense of who this person is rather than one snapshot.

I iterated on a cast of six characters, tracking which techniques produced consistent identity versus which ones caused drift.

Findings

What actually happened

Consistency is achievable, but it requires active maintenance. The moment you change lighting dramatically, or put a character in an unusual emotional state, or dress them in something very different from your reference set — drift happens. The model re-interprets.

What doesn't work: using a single reference image and expecting it to hold. The model is averaging across its reference set, so one strong image produces one strong interpretation, not a character.

What does work: multiple references from different contexts, very precise written descriptions of face shape and specific features (eye spacing, jaw line, skin tone in particular terms), and being willing to discard generations that look right but feel wrong. The discard rate was higher than I expected — maybe 70% of outputs were technically competent but didn't feel like the character.

The honest finding: AI character consistency is a craft skill. It takes judgment, iteration, and a clear internal sense of who the character is. That last part is the part the model can't do for you.
